\begin{abstract}
Feedback perimeter control utilizing the Macroscopic Fundamental Diagram (MFD) is a scalable approach to metropolitan congestion management, yet empirical deployment is constrained by sparse probe penetration ($1\%$--$5\%$), topological corridor selection bias, GPS multipath noise, and telemetry pipeline latency. This work formulates an integrated, pre-registered estimation-and-control architecture comprising four defensive layers: (i) a stateful kinematic quality gate that evaluates vehicle kinematics strictly against the last accepted valid ping per vehicle; (ii) a stratified link-class Horvitz--Thompson Edie estimator anchored by boundary detector counts; (iii) a two-branch hysteresis physics-constrained adaptive Kalman filter (PC-AKF) whose observation covariance scales with the gate rejection rate; and (iv) a residual-corrected Smith-PI perimeter controller that rolls the nonlinear MFD plant model forward across the telemetry delay and provably degenerates to a tuned normalized PI controller at zero delay. The framework is evaluated on an $8\times 8$ two-reservoir SUMO grid across \EvalSeedCount{} held-out evaluation seeds under a strict calibration/evaluation seed firewall, and against empirical urban trajectory benchmarks from Amsterdam's municipal road network. All reported numbers are injected mechanically from a versioned result artifact, and a Python--TypeScript parity harness ($\le 10^{-9}$) enables independent client-side verification of every quantity.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
Urban traffic networks experience capacity drop and gridlock when vehicular accumulation exceeds critical density \cite{daganzo2007urban,geroliminis2008existence}. Perimeter control regulates flow across reservoir boundaries to maintain accumulation near optimal production thresholds \cite{keyvan2012exploiting,haddad2012stability,geroliminis2013optimal}. Practical implementations, however, depend on floating vehicle data exhibiting low penetration and severe spatial bias---clustering along arterial corridors while leaving local streets unmonitored \cite{ambuhl2016data,du2016deriving,nagle2014accuracy}.

Four failure modes threaten the validity of MFD-based feedback control under such conditions: (1) GPS multipath outliers corrupting vehicle kinematics; (2) corridor selection bias distorting Edie-based network production estimates; (3) MFD parameter misspecification and hysteresis degrading filter tracking; and (4) telemetry pipeline latency destabilizing feedback loops \cite{normeyrico2008dead,yuan2023computational,keyvan2014perimeter}. Each defensive layer of the proposed architecture addresses exactly one of these modes, and each contribution is pre-registered before any sweep is executed \cite{nosek2018preregistration,munafo2017manifesto}.

\section{Framework: Four Defensive Layers}
\label{sec:framework}

\subsection{Layer 1: Stateful Kinematic Quality Gate}
For each vehicle trajectory, a sequential gate evaluates the candidate ping strictly against the \emph{last accepted valid ping} of the same vehicle, rather than against its immediate predecessor. A candidate ping is accepted only if the implied speed and displacement respect kinematic envelopes; gate rejections are recorded per vehicle with a bounded quarantine mechanism that prevents permanent blacklisting after transient multipath episodes.

\subsection{Layer 2: Stratified Link-Class Horvitz--Thompson Edie Estimator}
Gated trajectories are partitioned into link classes $c \in \{\text{arterial}, \text{local}\}$. Per class, Edie's generalized definitions \cite{edie1963discussion} integrate gated travel time and distance over the observation window, and the class accumulation is divided by a floor-protected class penetration estimate $\hat{p}_c$ anchored to boundary detector counts (screenline decomposition). Horvitz--Thompson weighting \cite{horvitz1952generalization} then combines the strata with class and total lane-kilometer weights, neutralizing the arterial over-representation that a single unstratified inverse-penetration correction would inherit.

\subsection{Layer 3: Two-Branch Hysteresis PC-AKF}
Accumulation is propagated by a mass-balance equation with a two-branch outflow function: a loading branch and an unloading branch with a reduced maximum, engaged when the accumulation estimate is decreasing while still congested \cite{gayah2011hysteresis,geroliminis2011b}. Observation covariance is inflated dynamically by the gate rejection rate $\rho_{\text{reject}}(t)$, so that sensor distrust translates directly into wider credible intervals \cite{kalman1960new,wang2005real,saeedmanesh2021extended}.

\subsection{Layer 4: Residual-Corrected Smith-PI Perimeter Control}
The controller synthesizes a normalized PI law over a Smith-type predictor \cite{smith1957closer,normeyrico2008dead}: the nonlinear MFD plant model is rolled forward across the buffered telemetry delay using the most recent buffered actuations, and a residual correction term blends the delayed observation against the model's delayed prediction. The law provably degenerates to a tuned normalized PI controller at zero delay and maintains genuine delay compensation at integer sub-window delays ($\tau_d = 60\,\text{s}$: one full rollout step) as well as at fractional sub-window delays ($\tau_d = 30\,\text{s}$: a half-window $\theta = 0.5$ rollout step interpolating between buffered observations).

\section{Pre-Registered Experimental Design}
\label{sec:design}
Simulations are conducted on an $8\times 8$ two-reservoir SUMO grid \cite{lopez2018microscopic} with a 90-minute hysteresis demand profile. The pre-registered plan (Section~\ref{sec:prereg}) fixes: probe penetration $\{1\%, 2\%, 5\%\}$; sampling topology \{uniform, corridor-biased $4{:}1$ arterial-to-local\}; telemetry delay $\tau_d \in \{0, 30, 60\}\,\text{s}$ with the fractional sub-window parameter $\theta = (\tau_d \bmod \Delta t)/\Delta t \in [0,1)$; and a $2\times 2\times 2$ ablation over estimation and control layers (G0/G1 $\times$ F0/F1 $\times$ C0/C1). Calibration seeds 1--10 tune controller gains exclusively; evaluation seeds 11--40 ($n=\EvalSeedCount$) are held out for paired non-parametric inference (Hodges--Lehmann median difference, Cliff's $\delta$, Wilcoxon signed-rank with Holm--Bonferroni adjustment) \cite{wilcoxon1945individual,hodges1963estimates,cliff1993dominance,holm1979simple,efron1993introduction}.

\section{Empirical Validation Scope (Amsterdam)}
\label{sec:amsterdam}
The pipeline is validated against empirical urban trajectory benchmarks drawn from Amsterdam's municipal road network (NDW open data) \cite{ndw2024amsterdam,knoop2013empirical,newson2009hidden}. \textbf{Scope statement:} the NDW benchmark is an \emph{open-loop estimation} validation---the replayed historical trajectories cannot be counterfactually actuated, i.e., \texttt{closed\_loop\_actuation\_supported=False}. Consequently, Amsterdam validation establishes estimation-layer accuracy (gate, stratified Edie, PC-AKF) against ground-truth loop counts, while closed-loop control claims (Layer~4) are established exclusively within the SUMO simulation environment where actuation is supported.
